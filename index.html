How it works

  1. Sun from Earth: Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, ch. 25 (low-precision solar position).
  2. Moon from Earth: Meeus ch. 47 — all 120 main terms for longitude, latitude and distance.
  3. J2000 coordinates: precession is removed (−1.3969713°·T from the longitude), then rotated from the ecliptic to Earth's equator.
  4. Moon-fixed frame: IAU 2009 lunar rotation model (pole direction α₀, δ₀ and spin angle W with the E1–E13 wobble terms).
  5. Local sky: at each site, "up", "east" and "north" vectors give elevation and azimuth. The Earth's direction is measured from the surface, not the Moon's centre (this lowers the Earth by ≈ 0.26° near the horizon).
  6. Statistics: the window is sampled every hour (every 3 h for 180 and 365 days). Sunlit = Sun centre above 0°. Earth visible = Earth elevation ≥ the comms minimum. Longest periods are the longest unbroken runs inside the window.

Limitations

  • Flat horizon: mountains and crater walls are ignored. At the pole, the Sun and Earth skim the horizon, so real terrain can block them often. Real numbers can be quite different.
  • Approximate site coordinates (to ~0.1°); the Sun is treated as a point (its disk is ≈ 0.53° wide, so partial sunlight is not modelled).
  • Accuracy: analytical formulas, good to about 0.01–0.05° for the Sun and Earth direction — far smaller than the effect of terrain.
